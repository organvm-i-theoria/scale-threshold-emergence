Below is the Lane Governance Charter, which formalizes how research lanes, sub-lanes, and inquiry threads are created, managed, merged, or retired. Its function is to stabilize the research architecture over long time horizons while allowing discovery to expand organically.



Lane Governance Charter v1


Framework for Managing the Research Architecture


Purpose


The charter defines rules for organizing research so that the system remains:


coherent
scalable
synthesis-oriented
resistant to fragmentation


Without governance, research systems drift into uncontrolled category proliferation.



1. Structural Levels


The research system operates on three structural levels.

Level
Role
Primary Lane
major threshold transition in the evolution of systems
Sub-Lane
focused research cluster within a lane
Inquiry Thread

Hierarchy:


primary lane
→ sub-lane
→ inquiry thread


Only the first level should be highly stable.



2. Primary Lane Definition


A primary lane represents a major transition in system complexity.


Characteristics:


long-term research domain

multiple mechanisms involved

3. Sub-Lane Definition


A sub-lane is a stable research cluster within a primary lane.


Characteristics:


narrower conceptual focus
distinct mechanism families
moderate literature base
expected to persist across many research cycles

Sub-lanes may expand but should remain bounded.



4. Inquiry Thread Definition


An inquiry thread represents a temporary investigation.


Characteristics:


specific question
limited literature
uncertain long-term relevance


Examples:


hydrothermal vent chemistry models
ritual memory reinforcement
peer review error correction


Threads are exploratory.


They should not become structural categories unless they repeatedly prove important.



5. Promotion Rules


Thread → Sub-Lane


A thread becomes a sub-lane if:


multiple independent sources address the same topic
a recurring mechanism family emerges
the topic persists across multiple research cycles


Minimum conditions:


≥10 relevant readings
≥2 mechanisms extracted



Sub-Lane → Primary Lane


Promotion to primary lane requires strong evidence.


Conditions:


addresses a new threshold transition
cannot be absorbed by existing lanes
has large canonical literature
supports multiple mechanism families


This should be rare.



6. Merger Rules


If two sub-lanes overlap heavily, they should merge.


Indicators:


same mechanisms
same literature sources
same research questions


Merge decision:


combine mechanism sets
retain most descriptive name
archive duplicate label



7. Retirement Rules


A lane, sub-lane, or thread may be retired if:


no new mechanisms emerge
research interest declines
the topic becomes absorbed by another lane


Retired categories remain in the archive but are no longer active.

9. Review Cycle


Governance decisions occur during periodic reviews.


Suggested cadence:

Review Type
Frequency
Thread evaluation
monthly

These reviews prevent uncontrolled expansion.



10. Governance Questions


Each review should ask:


Is this topic still producing mechanisms?
Does it deepen the threshold model?
Does it overlap with other lanes?


If the answer to all three is “no,” the category should be retired or merged.

12. Stability Principle


The charter follows one core rule:


structure must change more slowly than knowledge


If structural categories change faster than discoveries, the system loses coherence.



Result


With governance in place, the research architecture becomes:


stable at the top
flexible in the middle
experimental at the edges


Which is the ideal structure for a long-term research program.
