## Summary

### Main Thesis
"the energy that flows through a system acts to organize that system"

Living systems require a continuous flow of energy to maintain their organized state. The book applies thermodynamic principles to biological systems, arguing that life emerges from the non-equilibrium thermodynamics of energy flow through chemical systems.

### Key Claims
1. Living systems are thermodynamic machines dependent on energy flow
2. Order in biological systems is maintained through energy dissipation  
3. The origin of life can be understood through thermodynamics of open systems

## Key Contribution: Morowitz's Cycling Theorem

Morowitz discovered that "in steady state systems, the flow of energy through the system from a source to a sink will lead to at least one cycle in the system."

This is considered a candidate for a "fourth law of thermodynamics":
> "Morowitz's cycling theorem is the best candidate for a fourth law of thermodynamics" — Eric D. Schneider

## Notes

This foundational text establishes the thermodynamic perspective on life that Nick Lane later develops in "The Vital Question." The argument that life is fundamentally about energy flow, not information, provides a physics-first foundation for understanding the origin of life.

Morowitz's career spanned 50+ years working on the origin of life from a thermodynamic perspective. His work connects to:
- Santa Fe Institute (complex systems)
- Biosphere 2 (closed ecosystems)
- NASA (Apollo quarantine, Viking biology experiments)
- Krasnow Institute for Advanced Study

The cycling theorem provides a testable hypothesis: any system with sustained energy flow will develop cyclic processes.
