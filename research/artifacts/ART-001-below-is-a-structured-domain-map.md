# Below is a structured domain map.

Source pair: `TH-001`

## Prompt

Theory of Everything applies to which domains of reality?

## Response

A Theory of Everything (ToE), in the strict scientific sense, is a framework intended to unify all fundamental physical interactions under a single coherent mathematical system. However, the scope of “everything” depends on the ontological layer being considered. The concept can therefore be mapped across multiple domains of reality, from strictly physical to emergent or symbolic systems.


Below is a structured domain map.



1. Fundamental Physical Domain


This is the primary domain a scientific Theory of Everything is intended to address.


Scope


The unification of the four fundamental forces:

Force
Current Theory
Gravity
General Relativity
Electromagnetism
Quantum Electrodynamics
Weak Nuclear
Electroweak Theory
Strong Nuclear
Quantum Chromodynamics

Goal


A single mathematical framework describing:


space
time
matter
energy
fields
forces


Examples of candidate frameworks:


String theory

M-theory

Loop quantum gravity

Quantum geometric approaches

Unified field theories


This domain concerns the base rules of the universe.



2. Cosmological Domain


A ToE must extend to the large-scale structure of the universe.


Includes


origin of the universe

cosmic inflation

dark matter

dark energy

spacetime topology

multiverse hypotheses


The ToE would explain:


why the constants of physics exist
why the universe has its particular laws
how spacetime emerged



3. Quantum Information Domain


Many modern interpretations suggest reality is fundamentally informational.


Relevant fields include:


quantum information theory

holographic principle

AdS/CFT correspondence

quantum gravity entropy models


Key idea:


physical reality = information processes


This domain treats the universe as computational or informational substrate.



4. Chemical Domain


Chemistry is derivable from physics, but involves emergent complexity.


Includes:


atomic structure

molecular bonding

thermodynamics

reaction networks


A ToE would implicitly govern these but not necessarily simplify them due to combinatorial complexity.



5. Biological Domain


Biology emerges from chemistry but introduces self-organizing systems.


Includes:


evolution

genetics

metabolism

ecological systems

developmental biology


A ToE constrains biology but does not directly explain biological organization without additional theories (complex systems theory, evolutionary theory).



6. Cognitive Domain


Here reality becomes observer-dependent.


Includes:


perception

cognition

consciousness

neural information processing


Relevant theories intersect with:


neuroscience

computational theory of mind

integrated information theory

predictive processing


A physical ToE might provide constraints for consciousness but does not currently explain subjective experience.



7. Social Domain


Human systems emerge from cognition.


Includes:


economics

politics

institutions

culture

language


These domains involve:


symbol systems
game theory
collective dynamics
information networks


They are far removed from fundamental physics, though ultimately constrained by it.



8. Technological / Computational Domain


Artificial systems interacting with reality.


Includes:


computation

AI

cybernetics

digital networks

virtual worlds


These represent synthetic extensions of physical information systems.



9. Symbolic / Mythic Domain


Humans encode reality through symbolic systems.


Examples:


mythology

narrative

art

religion

philosophy


These domains operate on meaning structures, not physical laws.


However they still emerge from:


cognition
culture
communication networks



Domain Hierarchy of Reality


A practical hierarchical model looks like:


Symbolic systems
↑
Social systems
↑
Cognitive systems
↑
Biological systems
↑
Chemical systems
↑
Physical systems
↑
Quantum fields / spacetime


A strict Theory of Everything applies directly only to the bottom layer, but indirectly constrains all layers above.



Important Distinction


There are two meanings of “Theory of Everything”.


Physics Meaning


A unified mathematical description of fundamental forces.


Philosophical Meaning


A framework explaining all aspects of reality, including:


consciousness

meaning

culture

knowledge


No existing theory achieves this.

If useful, the next step is to formalize a universal domain taxonomy of reality—an exhaustive ontology of all systems from quantum fields to mythological narrative structures. That type of classification is often useful when designing large conceptual frameworks or system architectures.
